## Resumen

Fuente tipificada como [on_topic, oa, fulltext]. Síntesis del abstract/contenido disponible: Families presenting for therapy after a divorce or separation who are in high‐conflict present marriage and family therapists (MFTs) with some of the most complex and ethically challenging cases. Most co‐parents settle disagreements amicably; however, 8–15% engage in conflict for years post‐separation. Therapists must navigate overlapping clinical, legal, regulatory, and systemic domains—tasks for which most MFTs receive little training. This special issue of the Journal of Marital and Family Therapy addresses the urgent need for therapist competency and interdisciplinary collaboration with families in high conflict.
